### Buttons in the toolbar

### Selecting the Timeline View options


Timeline View Options: These controls let you tailor how clips look on the tracks (Filmstrip, Thumbnail, or Minimized), the audio waveform displays, stacked timelines, subtitles, and the adjustable height of both the video and audio tracks. Keyframe Editor: Opens the Keyframe Editor within the timeline. Voiceover: Opens the Voiceover tools for recording straight into the timeline. Selection Mode: The standard mode where you can shift and resize clips in the Timeline, roll edits, and carry out other basic editing chores. While in this mode, picking out particular selections in the Timeline and then invoking the Comma and Period nudge commands will resize, move, or roll what you selected, as will entering absolute or relative timecode. Trim Edit Mode: Here the Trim tool lets you perform slip, slide, ripple, and roll edits by dragging various parts of clips in the Timeline, by making particular selections and pressing the “nudge” keyboard shortcuts of Comma and Period to shift the selection left or right, or by making particular selections and using timecode entry to apply relative or absolute changes. Dynamic Trim Mode: This mode operates alongside either the Selection or Edit modes. Once Dynamic Trim mode is on, the JKL keyboard shortcuts that drive playback forward and backward through the Timeline give you, while in Selection mode, the ability to resize plus reposition clips, and while in Trim mode, the ability to ripple, slip, or slide them. With it enabled, the spacebar fires the Play Around Current Selection command. This mode’s Toolbar button likewise shifts to show whether, for nudging, timecode-entry adjustment, or dynamic trim, you are currently set to slip or to slide (which you choose by pressing the S key). Blade Edit Mode: Lets you drop cuts onto clips at the playhead in the Timeline with a click. Insert Clip: Carries out an insert edit to the Timeline using whatever clip sits in the Source Viewer. Overwrite Clip: Carries out an overwrite edit to the Timeline using whatever clip sits in the Source Viewer. Replace Clip: Carries out a replace edit to the Timeline using whatever clip sits in the Source Viewer. Snapping: Switches clip snapping on or off. With it active, clip In and Out points, markers, and the playhead all snap together for reference as you edit. Linked Selection: Switches audio/video linking on or off. With it active, clicking a video clip in the Timeline automatically grabs the matching audio clip when the two are linked. With it off, clicking a video clip leaves its audio unselected. As you work, holding the Option key while you click to select in the Timeline toggles clip linking. Position Lock: Stops clips from being shifted left or right and blocks every ripple operation. In effect it keeps all the Timeline elements in sync and guards against accidental changes.

Flag Clip/Flag Colors drop-down menu: Flags mark clips and point out every clip that maps to the same item of media in the Media Pool. A clip can carry several flags. Clicking the Flag button drops a flag automatically onto whatever clip happens to be selected at that moment in the Timeline. Off to the right, a drop-down menu lets you choose flags in assorted colors and strip every flag off the clip presently selected. Add Marker/Marker Colors drop-down menu: Markers pinpoint specific frames within individual clips. Clicking the Add Marker button drops a marker in the currently shown color onto the clip at the playhead’s position in the Timeline. A drop-down menu off to the right lets you pick markers of different colors and clear every marker from the currently selected clip. Full Extent Zoom: Adjusts the zoom level on the fly to take in the entire Timeline as you add or pull clips. Detail Zoom: Zooms the Timeline in on the Playhead down to the frame level. Custom Zoom: Zooms the Timeline to whatever level the Zoom slider to its immediate right is set to. Zoom Slider: Zooms you in toward or back from the clips sitting in the Timeline. Spin your mouse’s scroll wheel to zoom the Timeline horizontally. Scrolling up zooms in, while scrolling down zooms out. Pressing Command-Plus also zooms in, Command‑Minus zooms back out, and Shift-Z squeezes every clip in your program into the Timeline’s available width. The sections later in this chapter describe these functions in greater detail.
