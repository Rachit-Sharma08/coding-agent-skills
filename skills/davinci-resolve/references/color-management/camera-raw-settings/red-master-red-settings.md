### RED

R3D source media, captured by the assorted models in the RED DIGITAL CINEMA camera lineup, holds one of the richest collections of raw parameters among any of the camera formats. These settings split across four separate groups.

### Master RED Settings

The Master RED settings carry the most weight, governing decode quality plus the control that decides whether the original camera metadata gets used or whether you're overriding the camera metadata project-wide with custom settings. These settings also hold the drop-down menus letting you pick the color space and gamma curve that turn the raw image data into image data for processing in DaVinci Resolve as R3D clips get debayered. Whichever Color Space and Gamma Curve settings you settle on come down purely to preference; nothing strictly demands one or the other for any particular kind of workflow. You're merely after settings that hand you the best starting point for the media in hand, given the kind of grading you have in mind. As an example, pairing the REDcolor3 Color Space setting with the REDlog Film gamma curve will, in plenty of cases, give a starting point holding onto the most image detail along with the widest latitude for adjustment. By contrast, when you're pressed for time, say to turn out dailies for offline editing, a REDcolor Color Space setting teamed with a REDgamma setting can hand you an image that pleases right away and calls for fewer adjustments to land an acceptable result. None of this counts as a recommendation; they're examples only. As ever, the right settings for your project lean heavily on the source media's quality, so it pays to experiment with media drawn from your own projects to land the results that suit your eye best.

### Master

These top settings set the image quality you're opting to pull out of the R3D source media. The catch is that higher quality media at higher resolution taxes the processor more during debayering, subject to your workstation's capabilities.

- Decode Quality: Sets the image quality of the decoded R3D data passed along to the DaVinci Resolve image processing pipeline. Whichever Decode Quality you pick weighs directly on real time performance. Decoding performance hinges entirely on your system's hardware capabilities. On the newest systems, accelerated GPU-based debayering can decode R3D files, so long as the Use GPU for R3D drop-down menu is set to Debayer within the DaVinci Resolve System Settings' Decode Options panel. With DaVinci Resolve 16.1.2 came the latest RED API, which opened up 8K-accelerated debayering through Cuda. Failing that, R3D files can be decoded at high performance through multi-core CPU processing as long as your workstation packs fast enough CPUs.

Where needed, you can also pick a lower quality setting that yields better real time playback on systems with limited performance as you work, then move up to a higher quality once you render the final output. Following such a workflow is made simple by a "Force debayer res to highest quality" checkbox, found within the Deliver page's Render Settings list.

- Bit Depth: DaVinci Resolve is able to decode R3D files carrying 8-, 10-, or 16-bit image data for processing. Going with 16-bit for top quality can weigh on playback performance on certain hardware.
- Timecode: Whatever timecode got recorded for R3D media rests on the camera setting active when it was shot. Three choices exist:

Camera: This setting picks between Absolute and Edge on its own, going by whichever was set as the camera's default timecode mode. You need this setting chosen before you bring R3D media into the Media Pool. Should you be browsing R3D media as you change this setting, refresh the folder in the Library of the Media Pool before bringing media into the Media Pool. Absolute: The default. Captures "time of day" timecode. Had an external timecode source been hooked up with the camera placed in Jam Sync mode, the external timecode would have been captured in its place. Edge: For each magazine the first recorded clip opens at 01:00:00:00, with each clip after it timecoded in sequence and without break.

- Decode Using: Whichever option you pick sets whether every bit of R3D media across the project gets decoded through the original Camera Metadata settings (the default pick), through Project settings where you assign custom settings to apply across all clips, or through the RED default settings.

### Project Settings

These settings steer the core methods used to debayer R3D media. The picks you make here set the basic color and contrast you're opting to pull out of the camera raw image data.

- Color Science: The options run Original, the color science used by early builds of the REDone camera, then Version 2, and finally IPP2, the current color science across the whole RED camera line. Unless you have to match the look of older projects on the older color science, the newest color science is usually the better pick.
- Color Space: Since RED cameras capture R3D data in a raw color space, debayering the native R3D data means picking a color space to turn the raw signal into. Keep in mind the color space you pick is no more than a starting point for further correction. Nothing demands you pick one color space over another for any given workflow. Go with the color space that hands you the most pleasing starting point for the particular project at hand.

DragonColor2: A more finely tuned take on DragonColor, particularly suggested for underwater footage. REDcolor4: A more finely tuned take on REDcolor3, likewise suggested above all for underwater footage. REDWideGamutRGB: Belonging to RED's IPP2 (image processing pipeline 2) initiative, this camera color space is built to span every color a RED camera can capture without clipping, and aims to hand all RED camera models one shared starting point, for ready grading into HDR or SDR workflows. Rec. 2020: Decodes into the standard color space the Rec. 2020 standard lays down for high definition video, UHD video, and beyond. Handy though you may find this option, it isn't required for programs heading out to video.

Rec. 709: Decodes into the standard color space the Rec. 709 standard lays down for high definition video. Handy though you may find this option, it isn't required for programs heading out to video. sRGB: Decodes into the standard color space the sRGB standard defines, the one typically used for computer display. Adobe1998: Decodes into Adobe's own take on the sRGB standard. DCI-P3: Decodes into RGB-encoded image data carrying a D61 white point, meant for use when putting out media for DCI mastering. DCI-P3 D65: Decodes RGB-encoded image data carrying a D65 white point, meant for monitoring on a P3-compatible display. ProPhoto RGB: A color space Kodak developed offering a wide gamut aimed at photography. A quirk of this color space is that its green and blue primary points sit beyond the bounds of visible color, so the gamut takes in "imaginary" colors to reach an exceptionally large gamut. CameraRGB: Puts out the original, untouched sensor data. This setting isn't advised. REDspace: Packs the raw R3D image data into a color space wider than that of Rec. 709. Suited to digital cinema mastering and film output. REDspace came before the REDcolor setting. REDcolor: A color space close to the Rec. 709 option, but reworked to weigh accuracy against pleasing color rendition, with a focus on accurate skin tones. REDcolor2: Like REDcolor, though less saturated. REDcolor3: Saturation on par with REDcolor, yet with further tweaks to sharpen the color rendition of skin tone. Brought in as the prime color space for Epic cameras, though it suits earlier RED camera generations too. DragonColor: A color space tuned for cameras carrying the RED Dragon sensor, though this color space serves earlier RED camera generations as well.

- Gamma Curve: Several options exist for picking a gamma profile to apply while debayering the raw R3D data:

REDgamma4: The newest take on the REDgamma curve, built to deliver a solid in-camera look with no grading needed, all while holding onto great dynamic range and highlight handling. REDgamma4 suits every RED camera. REDlog Film: A refined logarithmic gamma setting built to remap the original 12-bit R3D data onto the standard Cineon gamma curve. The setting yields flat-contrast image data that holds onto image detail with wide latitude for adjustment, and it plays well with log workflows, those bound for film output included. Linear: No gamma adjustment happens; this is a linear-to-light rendering of the data off the RED camera's sensor. Rec. 709: A gamma curve typical of Rec. 709 display. Doesn't hand you much latitude for grading. Gamma 2.4: A plain power-function gamma setting common in broadcast. Gamma 2.6: A plain power-function gamma setting common in digital cinema projection. sRGB: A gamma setting close to the one Rec. 709 uses. HDR ST.2084: A standardized gamma curve covering high-dynamic-range (HDR) video. It also goes by the PQ curve. Hybrid Log Gamma: A standardized gamma curve covering the HLG standard of high-dynamic-range (HDR) video, which the BBC and NHK developed together.

BT.1886: A standardized gamma curve covering standard-dynamic-range HD and UHD display. Doesn't hand you much latitude for grading. Log3G12: An expanded option within RED's IPP2 (image processing pipeline 2) initiative; this wide dynamic range log space is built to encode camera data from every RED model onto a shared starting point in RWG color space, for ready grading into HDR or SDR workflows. Log3G12 hands you 12 stops of dynamic range above mid gray, 2 stops more than Log3G10. That said, it comes at the cost of a slight loss of precision. Log3G10: Belonging to RED's IPP2 (image processing pipeline 2) initiative, this is a broad dynamic range log space that encodes data from all RED models down to one common RWG-color-space starting point, handy for grading toward HDR or SDR workflows. Here, 3G points to mapping 18% mid gray onto 1/3, while 10 points to the 10 stops of dynamic range above mid gray it backs. PDlog 685: A logarithmic gamma setting mapping the native 12-bit RED image data onto the linear portion of a Cineon or film transfer curve. PDlog 985: The same kind of logarithmic gamma setting, only with different mappings. Custom PDlog: A logarithmic gamma setting exposing adjustable Black Point, White Point, and Gamma PDlog parameters that let you shape a log gamma curve of your own. REDspace: Close to Rec. 709, though tweaked slightly to be more appealing, chiefly through higher contrast and lighter midtones. The forerunner of the REDgamma curve. REDlog: A logarithmic gamma setting mapping the original 12-bit R3D image data onto a 10-bit curve. The blacks and midtones filling the video signal's lowest 8 bits keep the same precision they had in the original 12-bit data, while the highlights filling the topmost 4 bits get compressed. Though it trims the precision of highlight detail, the payoff is plentiful precision across the rest of the signal. It's a solid setting for holding onto maximum latitude. REDgamma: A refined gamma curve built to look perceptually pleasing on displays calibrated for Rec. 709, with a refined soft roll-off in the highlights that holds onto highlight detail through grading. REDgamma2: Like REDgamma, with higher contrast. REDgamma3: The most current take on the REDgamma curve. It builds off a log starting point but lays on a pleasing "ready to view" contrast curve, aiming to be a visually pleasing starting point that holds onto excellent dynamic range. REDgamma3 is also built to pair with REDcolor3.

- Blend Type: Governs how RED HDRX media gets used. When you lean on either Simple or Magic Motion to blend HDRX exposures, the second output in the Node Editor isn't needed. Three options are open to you:

None: Only the regular exposure gets used. Simple: Blends the two HDRX exposures into a pleasing middle ground. Magic Motion: Leans on a proprietary algorithm to fuse the dual exposures, merging the picture's overexposed and well-exposed regions in a more targeted way; meanwhile the regularly exposed source's sharpness gets blended against the underexposed source's motion blur.

- Blend Bias: Lets you set how much of the regular exposure and how much of the underexposure get combined.
- Apply Metadata Curves: Should the R3D media files have been preprocessed in REDCINE X Pro and saved carrying color curve metadata, this setting lets you either honor or drop that metadata.
- D.E.B. (Dragon Enhanced Blacks): A checkbox switching on the removal of red noise in RED cameras that carry the Dragon sensor.
- Embedded Audio: Switches on embedded audio in R3D media.
