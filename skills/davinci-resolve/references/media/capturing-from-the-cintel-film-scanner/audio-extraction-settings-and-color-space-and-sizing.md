### Audio Extraction Settings

As a rule, once the film type is chosen, DaVinci Resolve's automatic features will pull your optical audio cleanly. Still, the state of the optical track shifts with the state of the loaded film, and now and then that throws the automation off. When that happens, you can sidestep the automatic features and adjust by hand.

To go manual, just open the 'Audio Extraction' settings window by clicking 'Show Cintel Audio Settings' under the inspector options up near the top right of the viewer.

Audio extraction settings let you make the following manual adjustments:

Where they're called for, manual tweaks are available through the Audio Extraction settings. Show audio scan area This checkbox flips the audio scan area guides on or off. The guides show up as a box on the frame's edge spanning the optical audio scan area, revealing which optical information feeds the extraction. Where the guides land follows the film type you've chosen. Even so, you can reposition them by hand when needed. The audio scan area guides also serve as handy signals of what's going on through extraction, letting you spot any trouble brewing and step in manually. A slim red line runs inside the box. That line is the mid point detector, which picks up the divide between stereo audio channels. When mono sound turns up during audio extraction, the mid point detector vanishes and the guides resize on their own to fit the width of the mono optical track. TIP: For a tighter look at the audio scan area guides, zoom into the viewer and shift the viewer position up or down and left or right. Simply set your zoom amount from the sizing options at the viewer's top left corner, then drag the viewer around by clicking and holding with the mouse or track pad.

With the 'show audio scan area' setting switched on, the audio area guides come into view so you can see precisely which information is feeding the process and follow the extraction along. Override audio scan area This setting hands you sliders to set the horizontal and vertical placement, width, and height of the audio scan area guides. These settings include:

- Left and Width: When your film type puts audio on the right side of the frame, you can just nudge the 'left' slider to shift the guide box rightward. As a rule this happens by itself once the matching film type is chosen, but the setting gives you extra room to adjust when you want it. In the same way, the 'width' setting handles the width of the scan area. These come in useful for fine-tuning the side edges of the guide box whenever there are unwanted elements within the film's optical audio area. Such intrusions can stem from perforation wear and tear, or from uneven print quality, and they may at times disrupt the quality of the audio extraction. A small nudge to the side edges, keeping the stray elements outside the guide box, helps you steer clear of this.
- Top: This setting adjusts the vertical position of the guide box.
- Height: On older rolls, the film frames can run a touch smaller than usual owing

to shrinkage that builds up over the years. As you hand-set the guide box, the 'height' slider is where you compensate for that film shrinkage.

- Auto adjust audio scan height: On by default, this setting moves the guide box height on its own to line up with the audio waveform at the top of each frame. The automatic behavior holds up well under normal audio conditions; however, if during extraction you see the box wander at random and the extraction quality suffer, the cause may be similar audio-track features overlapping from one frame to the next. Should that crop up, clear the checkbox and run the extraction again. When you clear the 'Auto adjust audio scan height' checkbox, confirm the 'height' setting parks the guide box at the best spot for the frame. Manual adjustments can rescue you when needed, but remember to flip the automatic features back on once you're through!

TIP: Should you turn off the 'Auto adjust audio scan height' checkbox, see that the 'height' setting seats the guide box at the best spot for the frame. Hand adjustments are there to help when you need them, just be sure to switch the automatic features back on once you're finished!

- Audio waveform color is white: Depending on which film type was scanned, the audio waveform can read black or white. When it's white, confirm the matching checkbox is on. That makes sure the white information in the waveform feeds the audio extraction. If instead the waveform is black against a white audio area, leave the checkbox off so DaVinci understands it should use the black information in the waveform. Getting this setting right also underpins other automatic features like mid point and mono detection.
- Override firmware stability: Once in a while, the film's condition will have introduced large frame movements through the internal firmware stabilization. That can knock the audio extraction guide box out of line with the optical track. Should it happen, turning on 'override firmware stability' lets the audio extraction guide box follow the film perforations on its own and shift its placement for possibly better results.
- Variable density audio: When your film holds variable density audio, be sure to tick the 'Variable density audio' checkbox so DaVinci Resolve knows which audio type to extract. The default sits at 'off' for variable area audio soundtracks. If variable density audio is new to you, you can spot it by eye as a packed run of shaded lines, much like a bar code with its lines pressed nearer together. For contrast, 'variable area' soundtracks look like an audio waveform.

### Color Space and Sizing

Two 1D LUTs, 'Cintel Negative to Linear,' and 'Cintel Print to Linear,' ship with the software to ease moving scanned media into a color space where you can keep working. Apply these LUTs through a node on the 'color' page to bring the original scans into a Linear color space. That said, when you want the image in Rec. 709 or in Cineon for further work, plan to apply a second LUT in a second node. Print defaults to a 2.2 gamma standard log curve, while everything else uses 2.046 film density log gamma. As a general rule with negative film, "color invert" after the second LUT goes on. Beyond that, the Linear data usually needs some grading to clear out black offsets caused by Dmin so the conversion into the target color space comes out right. A range of VFX IO LUTs sits in the 3D LUT submenu of every node's contextual menu, letting you move an image from Linear color space into any other color space you'd like to work within.

A three-node chain handles the film-scan conversion via LUTs; node 1 takes Negative or Print into Linear, node 2 carries Linear over to Rec. 709, and node 3, where called for, inverts the color NOTE: A LUT placed inside a node will clip whatever image data falls below 0 or above 1. To head off that clipping, reach for the Lift/Gamma/Gain controls in any node carrying a LUT and set your image levels before the LUT in that node performs its transform. Both the format of the film you're scanning and how the material was originally shot have a bearing on the framing. Reframing a scanned clip is open to you through resizing, zooming, stretching, panning, tilting, and other moves besides. Head to the 'Color' page, bring up the 'Sizing' palette, and lean on the 'Input Sizing' mode to assemble the framing you're after. To keep your sizing choices as a preset, open the menu, choose 'save as new preset' and type a name for your preset. After you've built a suitable sizing preset for a particular kind of media, you can apply it across a group of film scans in one go — from either the Color page or the Media Pool — using the 'change input sizing preset' command sitting in the contextual menu of the clips you've selected. For more information on sizing, see Chapter 152, "Sizing and Image Stabilization."
