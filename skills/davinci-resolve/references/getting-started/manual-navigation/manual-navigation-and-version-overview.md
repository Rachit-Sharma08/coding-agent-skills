### Why Is This Manual So Big?

Across the years, DaVinci Resolve has expanded well beyond the grading tools that formed the original core of DaVinci Resolve to take in full professional editing, compositing, and audio mixing tools and workflows. Every one of these functional domains runs remarkably deep. As a result, the documentation has swelled alongside each fresh page, tool, and parameter introduced, all meant to smooth the way and to address the endless issues that surface throughout postproduction. We wish this manual did not hold quite such an overwhelming volume of detail, yet our priority has always been making sure that (ideally) every control and workflow you meet in DaVinci Resolve is documented somewhere across these pages. With that in mind, we trust the hyperlinked table of contents (TOC) and the search tools in whatever PDF browser you favor will help you locate what you need, together with the context and tips that let you draw the most from what the tools offer.

### Using the Color Page

Because DaVinci Resolve began life as a professional grading tool, the Color page remains central to the whole DaVinci Resolve experience. The Color page holds every control you need for working color and contrast, cutting noise, setting up limited secondary color corrections, crafting assorted image effects, reshaping clip geometry, and applying countless other corrective and stylistic tweaks. This chapter walks you through reading the Color page interface, tailoring it, and operating inside it to play back and move around your project’s timeline. You’ll additionally pick up how to examine and contrast clips ahead of grading by way of stills, playheads, and the internal video scopes that ship with DaVinci Resolve.

### Dual-Monitor Layout

A dual monitor layout is available on the Color page, giving the Viewer, Node Editor, and Control palettes the fullest possible room on the primary monitor while the Gallery, Lightbox, Keyframe Editor, Metadata Editor, and Video Scope panel show at the same time on the secondary monitor. You can resize that second screen of the dual-screen layout, but its arrangement is fixed. Even with a single ultra-wide monitor you may still adopt the dual-monitor layout, and DaVinci Resolve scales the interface as though two ordinary monitors stood next to each other. To switch into dual-screen mode: Choose Workspace > Dual Screen > On.
